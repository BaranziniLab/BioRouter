The light theme,
in UCSF teal.

Alma Mater's dark mode is a confident navy instrument. Its light mode is not — it is a near-colourless blue-grey that reads as unfinished rather than institutional. This document measures exactly why, rebuilds the surfaces around UCSF's teal column, and validates every token against the same 35 contrast assertions the repo's guard enforces — computed live, in this page, for every option you can pick above.

The structure stays grey and the canvas stays white — that part of the theme was right, and it is what every peer institution ships. What changes is that the grey stops being violet grey, the sidebar's steps become visible, and UCSF teal arrives where the eye actually goes: the nav icons, the active rail, the primary action and links.

One caveat up front, in §02: the brand research does not support treating teal as co-iconic with navy. Navy anchors the identity; teal is one of six peer hues and, on UCSF's own site, a micro-accent. Keeping teal to icons and actions rather than to whole surfaces is the more literal reading of UCSF's one stated colour rule — and the stronger design.

What is actually wrong with the light theme

measured from the shipping tokens, not inferred

The complaint — "the light blueish grey doesn't contrast well" — is reproducible as three separate numeric defects. None of them is a contrast-guard failure, which is why the theme passes CI and still looks wrong.

A

The grey is violet, which is why it reads "blueish"

The sidebar sits at hue 258° and the active row at C 0.0081 — enough chroma for a violet cast to register on a large flat field, not enough to look like a decision. That in-between state is what the eye rejects. The fix is not more colour here: it is less. At C ≈ 0.002 no hue resolves at all and the surface reads as a clean neutral grey.

NowC 0.0046–0.0081 @ hue 254–258° — violet cast
NewC ≈ 0.002 — a true neutral

B

The brand never appears where the eye goes

Structure staying grey is correct — every peer institution measured does exactly that. What is missing is the small, saturated brand dose those peers pair it with. Today the only UCSF colour in the light theme is the CTA button. The nav icons — the most-looked-at graphics in the app — are plain navy.

Nowbrand = one button
Newbrand = icons + rail + button + links

Two surface steps are below the perception floor

Sidebar hover moves the surface by only ΔL* 2.85. Under about ΔL* 3 a surface change is not reliably seen at all, so hovering a nav row appears to do nothing. Note the inset ladder (I6→J5→K3) is already well spaced at 4.21/5.65/5.73 — the flatness is specific to the sidebar.

The accent is a UCSF colour, but not the UCSF colour

Eggplant is genuinely in the brand matrix (G2) and was chosen to dodge the "corporate blue" cliché. But nobody pictures plum when they picture UCSF — they picture navy and teal. The twist cost the theme its recognisability.

02

What the brand research actually found

including one finding that argues against this redesign's premise

Before choosing hexes, the UCSF brand was re-derived from primary sources — the lettered matrix at identity.ucsf.edu/brand-guide/color parsed from raw HTML, plus the computed styles of live UCSF properties. Four findings changed the design. The first one is uncomfortable and is reported here rather than buried.

!

Teal is not co-iconic with navy

The brief assumed teal is "maybe just as iconic as navy". The evidence does not support that. Navy A1 is the single base colour, is UCSF's body-text ink, and is the only colour with a stated proportion rule. Teal C3 is one of six peer primary hue ranges, and across 382 KB of live ucsf.edu CSS the teal token is applied in three declarations. Teal is real, official and beautiful — but it is a micro-accent, not a co-anchor.

identity.ucsf.edu/brand-guide/color · measured CSS
1

So teal is used where it is strongest

This is why the proposal makes teal the accent and leaves navy as the ink, the inverse surface and the whole dark mode — rather than turning the app teal. UCSF's one proportion rule is "pair navy as a base colour with neutrals and up to three additional colours". Navy base + one teal accent is a literal reading of that sentence.

brand guide, colour usage
2

#F2F3F4 is not an arbitrary grey

The ground that reads as washed out is I6 — and I6 is the measured page background of www.ucsf.edu. The current theme was not wrong to pick it. It fails here because a marketing page surrounds it with navy bands and full-bleed photography, while a dense desktop app surrounds it with more grey. The same swatch, different context.

computed body background, www.ucsf.edu
3

Peers never put a large field in the mid-tones

Across Stanford Medicine, Hopkins, Mayo, Broad, Dana-Farber and Cleveland Clinic, brand colour appears either very dark (L* < 25) or small and fully saturated — never as a large mid-tone, mid-saturation field. That zone is the washed-out blue-grey. Hopkins Medicine, navy + teal exactly like UCSF, is the closest analogue.

Ruletint stays under C 0.026 on big surfaces
measured computed styles, 8 peer sites
4

UCSF publishes its ratios against navy, not white

The brand guide lists C3 Teal at 5.08:1 — that is against navy. Against white it is 3.15:1 and fails AA for body text. Copying UCSF's published numbers into a light-theme argument would have shipped an inaccessible link colour. Every ratio in this document is computed here, against the actual token.

verified numerically · §5 below
5

There is a sanctioned ultra-light tint tier

UCSF's Sitebuilder theme ships an unpublished "6" row — B6 #E2F4FC, C6 #E8F6F8, A6 #E6EEF6 — ultra-light tinted panels. It is precedent that UCSF itself tints surfaces toward brand hues rather than leaving them neutral grey. C6 is the heatmap's idle step here.

pharm.ucsf.edu theme CSS · not brand-guide policy

Components and data surfaces

where the accent earns its keep — and where it must stay out

Teal appears on primary actions, the active-nav rail, live-status dots and links. It does not appear on focus (a neutral surface shift, D-15), on status (the alert columns own those hues), or as decoration.
